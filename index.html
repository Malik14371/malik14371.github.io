Understanding your numbers

What is BMI, and what does it actually tell you?

Body Mass Index compares your weight to your height to give a rough estimate of whether you're in a typical weight range for your height. It doesn't measure body fat directly, and it can be misleading for very muscular people or older adults who've lost muscle mass — but it's still one of the fastest, most widely used starting points doctors and researchers rely on.

What are BMR and TDEE?

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is roughly how many calories your body burns just staying alive — breathing, circulating blood, repairing cells — if you did nothing but rest all day. Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) adds your activity level on top of that, giving a realistic estimate of how many calories you actually burn in a normal day. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which most dietitians consider more accurate than older formulas.

What counts as a healthy "ideal weight"?

Ideal weight formulas — this tool uses the Devine formula — were originally built for medical dosing calculations, not as a personal goal. Treat the result as a reasonable reference range rather than a strict target: frame size, muscle mass, and overall body composition all matter more than a single number on a scale.

What does body fat percentage measure that BMI doesn't?

Body fat percentage estimates how much of your weight is actually fat versus muscle, bone, and water — the distinction BMI can't make. This calculator uses the U.S. Navy method (neck, waist, and for women, hip measurements), which is reasonably accurate for a method that needs no special equipment.

Frequently asked questions

Is this medical advice?

No. This tool gives general estimates based on standard formulas and is not a substitute for advice from a doctor, dietitian, or other qualified professional — especially if you're pregnant, an athlete, elderly, or managing a medical condition.

Why do my BMI and body fat percentage seem to disagree?

BMI only looks at height and weight, so a muscular person can show a "high" BMI while actually having low body fat. Body fat percentage is generally the more informative of the two for judging health risk.

How often should I recalculate?

Weight and body composition change slowly — recalculating every few weeks is plenty. Day-to-day changes are usually water weight, not fat.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. Every calculation happens directly in your browser. Nothing you enter is stored, logged, or sent to a server.

Which activity level should I pick for TDEE?

Be honest rather than optimistic — most people overestimate their activity level. If you're unsure, start with "Lightly active" and adjust based on how your weight trends over a few weeks.
